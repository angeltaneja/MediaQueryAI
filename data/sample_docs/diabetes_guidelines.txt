CLINICAL GUIDELINES: MANAGEMENT OF TYPE 2 DIABETES MELLITUS
==============================================================

Document Type: Clinical Practice Guideline
Issuing Authority: National Institute for Clinical Excellence (Adapted)
Last Updated: January 2024
Version: 4.2

1. INTRODUCTION
----------------
Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycemia resulting from insulin resistance and progressive beta-cell dysfunction. It affects approximately 462 million individuals worldwide, representing 6.28% of the global population. This guideline provides evidence-based recommendations for diagnosis, management, and monitoring of T2DM in adult patients.

2. DIAGNOSIS
-------------

2.1 Diagnostic Criteria
Diagnosis of T2DM requires one or more of the following:
- Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) on two separate occasions
- 2-hour plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) during an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) using 75g glucose load
- HbA1c ≥ 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) confirmed on repeat testing
- Random plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) in a patient with classic symptoms of hyperglycemia

2.2 Prediabetes Classification
- Impaired Fasting Glucose (IFG): FPG 100-125 mg/dL
- Impaired Glucose Tolerance (IGT): 2-hour glucose 140-199 mg/dL
- HbA1c 5.7-6.4%
Patients with prediabetes should receive lifestyle intervention counseling and annual monitoring.

3. PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT
-------------------------------

3.1 First-Line Therapy
Metformin remains the preferred initial pharmacological agent for T2DM:
- Starting dose: 500 mg once or twice daily with meals
- Titrate gradually to minimize GI side effects
- Maximum dose: 2000-2550 mg/day in divided doses
- Monitor renal function (eGFR) annually
- Contraindicated if eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m²
- Hold before iodinated contrast procedures

3.2 Second-Line Therapy
If HbA1c target is not achieved after 3 months of metformin monotherapy, consider adding:

a) SGLT2 Inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin)
   - Preferred in patients with established cardiovascular disease or heart failure
   - Additional benefits: weight loss, blood pressure reduction
   - Monitor for genital mycotic infections and diabetic ketoacidosis
   - Avoid if eGFR < 20 mL/min/1.73m²

b) GLP-1 Receptor Agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide)
   - Preferred in patients with established atherosclerotic CVD or high CV risk
   - Additional benefits: significant weight loss
   - Contraindicated in patients with personal/family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2
   - Common side effects: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea

c) DPP-4 Inhibitors (sitagliptin, saxagliptin, linagliptin)
   - Weight neutral
   - Well-tolerated, fewer GI side effects
   - Lower HbA1c reduction compared to GLP-1 RAs
   - Dose adjustment needed for renal impairment (except linagliptin)

d) Sulfonylureas (glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide)
   - Effective and low cost
   - Risk of hypoglycemia and weight gain
   - Glyburide not recommended in elderly
   - Monitor blood glucose closely during initiation

e) Thiazolidinediones (pioglitazone)
   - Improve insulin sensitivity
   - Risk of fluid retention, heart failure exacerbation, fractures
   - Contraindicated in NYHA Class III/IV heart failure

3.3 Insulin Therapy
Indications for insulin initiation:
- Symptomatic hyperglycemia with HbA1c > 10% or glucose > 300 mg/dL
- Failure to achieve glycemic targets on maximum oral/injectable therapy
- Type 1 diabetes misclassified as type 2

Basal insulin options:
- Insulin glargine (Lantus, Basaglar, Toujeo): once daily, usually at bedtime
- Insulin detemir (Levemir): once or twice daily
- Insulin degludec (Tresiba): once daily, flexible timing

Starting dose: 10 units or 0.1-0.2 units/kg/day
Titrate by 2-4 units every 3-7 days targeting fasting glucose 80-130 mg/dL

4. GLYCEMIC TARGETS
---------------------
- General adult population: HbA1c < 7.0%
- Older adults (≥65 years) with few comorbidities: HbA1c < 7.5%
- Older adults with multiple comorbidities or limited life expectancy: HbA1c < 8.0%
- Pregnant patients: fasting glucose < 95 mg/dL, 1-hour postprandial < 140 mg/dL

Self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG):
- On insulin: test fasting and before meals, minimum 2-4 times daily
- On oral agents only: individualized based on hypoglycemia risk
- Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) may be considered for insulin-treated patients

5. CARDIOVASCULAR RISK MANAGEMENT
------------------------------------
T2DM patients are at increased risk for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD).

5.1 Blood Pressure Targets
- Target: < 130/80 mmHg for most patients
- First-line agents: ACE inhibitors or ARBs (especially with albuminuria)
- May require combination therapy with calcium channel blockers or thiazide diuretics

5.2 Lipid Management
- High-intensity statin therapy for all patients aged 40-75 with diabetes
- Target LDL < 100 mg/dL (< 70 mg/dL for established ASCVD)
- Consider adding ezetimibe if LDL target not met on maximum statin
- PCSK9 inhibitors for very high-risk patients not at goal

5.3 Antiplatelet Therapy
- Aspirin 75-162 mg/day for secondary prevention in patients with established ASCVD
- Primary prevention: individualized risk-benefit assessment
- Dual antiplatelet therapy post-ACS as per cardiology guidelines

6. COMPLICATIONS SCREENING
-----------------------------

6.1 Diabetic Retinopathy
- Dilated eye exam at diagnosis, then annually
- More frequent if retinopathy detected
- Refer to ophthalmology for proliferative retinopathy or macular edema

6.2 Diabetic Nephropathy
- Annual urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) and serum creatinine/eGFR
- Start ACE inhibitor or ARB if UACR > 30 mg/g
- Consider finerenone for patients with CKD and T2DM on maximum RAAS blockade
- Refer to nephrology if eGFR < 30 or rapidly declining

6.3 Diabetic Neuropathy
- Annual comprehensive foot exam including monofilament testing
- Screen for symptoms: numbness, tingling, burning pain in feet
- First-line treatment for painful neuropathy: duloxetine, pregabalin, or gabapentin
- Patient education on daily foot inspection and proper footwear

6.4 Diabetic Foot Care
- Risk stratification at each visit
- Multidisciplinary team approach for active foot ulcers
- Offloading, wound care, vascular assessment, and infection management
- Patient education on prevention strategies

7. LIFESTYLE MODIFICATIONS
-----------------------------
- Medical nutrition therapy: individualized meal planning with registered dietitian
- Physical activity: minimum 150 minutes/week of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise
- Weight management: 5-10% weight loss improves glycemic control significantly
- Smoking cessation: strongly recommended, offer pharmacotherapy and counseling
- Alcohol: limit to ≤1 drink/day for women, ≤2 drinks/day for men
- Diabetes self-management education and support (DSMES) at diagnosis, annually, and during transitions

8. MONITORING SCHEDULE
------------------------
- HbA1c: every 3 months until at target, then every 6 months
- Lipid panel: annually or 6 weeks after medication change
- Renal function (eGFR, UACR): annually
- Liver function: at baseline and periodically
- Comprehensive metabolic panel: annually
- Dilated eye exam: annually
- Comprehensive foot exam: annually
- Blood pressure: every visit
- Weight and BMI: every visit
- Depression screening: annually (PHQ-9)
